Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about soil percolation, septic permits, and county standards.

A percolation test measures the rate at which water absorbs into the soil on your property. It tests whether the soil can effectively filter wastewater effluent from a septic tank without backing up or contaminating local groundwater.
A slow or failed initial perc test does not automatically mean you cannot build! Modern engineered septic systems—such as Sand Mound, Aerobic Treatment Units (ATU), or Drip Irrigation systems—can be installed on sites with clay soil or high water tables where standard gravity systems fail.
On-site testing generally takes between 4 to 8 hours (including soil presoak requirements mandated by health regulations). Stamped engineering reports and county paperwork are compiled and sent to you or the health department within 2 to 5 business days.
Most counties require a "Deep Hole Profile Pit" (typically 6-8 feet deep) alongside the shallow perc holes. We can arrange heavy equipment operators as part of your service package, or work directly with your site excavation crew.
